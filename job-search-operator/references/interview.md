# Prepare for an Interview

Use the workspace contract. Set `SKILL_ROOT` to the directory containing
`SKILL.md`; use its resolved `PROFILE`, `APPLICATIONS`, `APPLICATION`,
`NETWORK`, `POSITIONING`, and `PIPELINE` paths. Read the canonical profile,
the matching application record, and relevant network record before preparing.
Use only profile-supported candidate claims; do not invent scope, outcomes,
titles, tools, or relationships.

## Prepare the conversation

State the role priorities and evaluation criteria from the role snapshot and
job description. Identify each known interviewer, their role, and why their
perspective matters; mark unknown interviewers or relevance as unknown.

Prepare concise, truthful responses to the relevant question types:

- Recruiter: interest, availability, location or work arrangement, and terms.
- Hiring manager: priorities, ownership, operating context, and impact.
- Technical or functional: methods, tools, tradeoffs, and problem solving.
- Leadership: strategy, collaboration, decision making, and team enablement.
- Behavioral: specific situation, action, result, and learning.

Map each likely question to a verified profile story. Keep the original scope,
role, dates, and outcomes intact. For a gap, acknowledge it plainly, describe
the adjacent verified experience or learning plan, and do not imply it is
direct experience.

Prepare questions that clarify the goals, team, operating model, constraints,
and measures of success. Confirm logistics: stage, format, date and time,
time zone, interviewers, accessibility needs volunteered by the candidate,
and any preparation or materials requested. Do not infer a stage or outcome.

## Practice and debrief loop

Treat interviewing as a skill that improves through deliberate rehearsal, not
as a reason to pursue roles the candidate would not accept. Before a priority
process, rehearse the current positioning narrative, likely transitions, and
the highest-risk questions through a mock interview when practical.

After every real or mock interview, capture the questions asked, stories used,
points that landed, points that felt weak, new role facts, objections, and one
specific change for the next conversation. Rehearse the revised answer without
changing verified facts. Use repeated patterns across comparable conversations
to adjust positioning; do not overreact to one interviewer.

## Follow up and record

Draft, but do not send without authorization, a brief follow-up:

```text
Subject: Thank you — [role]

Thank you for discussing [specific topic]. I appreciated learning about
[team goal or constraint]. The conversation reinforced my interest in
contributing [profile-supported relevance]. Please let me know if I can
provide anything else.
```

After a confirmed interview stage, update the application log and pipeline
with the date, stage, result or status, debrief learning, and next action. Update the network
record when a contact relationship changes. Update positioning only when an
external signal changes an audience, claim, or message; never record a draft
or assumed progress as completed.
