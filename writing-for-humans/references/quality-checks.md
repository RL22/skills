# Human-quality checks

These checks require editorial judgment. Do not reduce them to word counts.

## Reader alignment

- Is the primary reader obvious?
- Is the reader's immediate problem specific?
- Is the desired outcome clear?
- Is the next decision or action clear?
- Does every section serve that reader and decision?

## Reasoning and structure

- Does the thesis, answer, or action appear early?
- Does each section have one visible job?
- Are claims connected by clear logic?
- Is the mechanism visible before the conclusion?
- Are abstractions grounded in observable behavior or examples?
- Does the piece use the simplest structure that fits?

## Human quality

- Does the writing sound spoken rather than assembled?
- Does sentence rhythm vary naturally?
- Are concrete nouns and active verbs doing the work?
- Is the language specific without becoming overexplained?
- Does the piece show judgment rather than generic enthusiasm?
- Does the ending earn its next step?

## Evidence and ethics

- Are material claims evidenced or clearly labeled?
- Are fact, inference, recommendation, anecdote, and hypothesis distinct?
- Are promises narrower than the evidence supports?
- Does the writing preserve reader agency?
- Does it avoid shame, humiliation, and psychological invalidation?
- Does it acknowledge meaningful constraints when relevant?

## Channel fit

- Does the structure fit the medium?
- Is the CTA appropriate and singular?
- Are formatting and density appropriate for the reader's context?
- Does the voice match Sprintz without forcing slogans or jargon?
